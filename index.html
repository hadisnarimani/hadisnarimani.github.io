Couched

Type
Fashion boutique
Stage
Concept design
Source
Couching embroidery

Couching is an embroidery technique: a cord is laid on the surface of the cloth and stitched down. COUCHED applies that rule to a shop.

The walls are pleated velvet, the ground cloth. Cord and sequin are laid over it and fixed only where a customer arrives, passes through or reaches: the entrance, the door frame, the rails and the fitting room.

At the entrance the laid cord becomes a loop of overlapping metal discs with a sequin lining. Inside, the same wrap reduces to the scale of a hanging rail.

Maille

Type
Hotel interiors, Dubai
Stage
Concept design
Source
Couture beadwork
Award
SBID International Design Awards 2026, Regional Winner, Middle East, CGI & Visualisation

One beadwork language, varied only in density and placement, gives each space its own character while holding the hotel together.

In couture beadwork, beads gather at points of emphasis and thin out elsewhere. In the hotel they concentrate at reception, expand across the bar wall, run as a directional band along the corridor and reduce to a single band in the spa.

Social spaces carry the most worked surface. Quiet spaces carry the least.

Kaolin

Type
Coastal hotel
Stage
Concept design
Source
Harris Reed, Autumn/Winter 2024

Kaolin is the clay porcelain is made from. The hotel treats painted blue-and-white ceramic as a building surface, not as decoration placed on one.

Cobalt flowers move between glazed tile panels and raised plaster relief. Circular openings frame the sea, and a chequered stone floor carries the same two colours from the arrival hall to the terrace and the boutique.

Laque

Type
Urban thermal house and collector's hotel
Stage
Concept design
Source
A wrapped, ombré couture piece

A garment that wraps the body in graded colour becomes a set of curved screens that wrap the room.

Lacquer and tinted glass turn around the seating, the bath and the stair, moving from amber through oxblood to aubergine. The surrounding walls stay matt and olive so the gloss reads as the only worked surface.

Rocaille

Type
Hotel and spa
Stage
Concept design
Source
Bead-loom couture

On a bead loom, an image is built from small units set in rows. Rocaille builds its walls the same way.

The unit here is the mosaic piece. Flowers are built square by square, with a bronze line running between them like the thread. The pattern changes scale from room to room: tall and loose in the reception hall, cut into panels along the corridor, fine and continuous from wall to floor around the pools.

Lapis

Type
Hotel
Stage
Concept design

Stone, walnut and nickel, with one colour held back for the details: lapis blue.

The public rooms are built as tall stone volumes lined in walnut joinery. Nickel frameworks carry the light, the glazing and the stair, and each one returns to the same stepped, faceted outline. Blue appears only in small, deliberate points: glass inside the lanterns, a stone set into a cabinet, vases on the shelves.

Palimpsest

Type
Boutique hotel
Stage
Concept design
Source
A white couture gown with a curved cut-out over beadwork

A plain surface is cut open to show a pattern underneath. The gown does this once; Palimpsest does it in every room.

New plaster walls stand inside an old stone building and are cut along a single curve. Through each cut, black and white mosaic shows like the beadwork under the gown, edged in a thin brass line. The cut becomes a mirror at the entrance, a headboard in the bedroom and a niche in the dining room.

Passementerie

Type
Boutique
Stage
Concept design

Passementerie is the craft of trimmings: fringe, cord, tassel and braid. Here the trimming becomes the joinery.

Fringe is drawn out at architectural scale as radiating rods set into a curved, upholstered wall. The same edge returns as cabinet pulls, as the rail of a bar pendant and as the fine lines inlaid in the floor. Plaster flowers climb the display walls, so every room carries one trim and one ornament.

Veil

Type
Bar and lounge
Stage
Concept design
Source
A sheer gown with three-dimensional appliqué flowers

On the gown, solid flowers sit on a transparent layer. Veil keeps that order: frosted glass first, flowers applied on top.

The bar fronts and window screens are made of frosted and tinted glass, lit from behind. Cast flowers are fixed to the surface, so they stay sharp while the room behind them softens. On the walls the same flowers grow larger and turn into light fittings.

Nacre

Type
Boutique and café
Stage
Concept design

Mother-of-pearl set into warm timber, from the shop floor to the café.

Walnut panelling, cabinets and the stair rail are inlaid with fine lines and sprays of mother-of-pearl. The inlay is kept to edges and borders, so the timber stays dominant and the pearl reads as a highlight under low light.

Pli

Type
Restaurant and bar
Stage
Concept design

Pli is French for fold. One folded form, oxblood outside and gold inside, moves through the restaurant.

Against teal plaster, the fold appears as a canopy over the bar, a screen beside the dining table, a sculpture over the stair and a frame around the washroom mirror. Each time the outer face is dark and the inner face catches the light.

Objects

Type
Furniture collection
Stage
Concept design
Material
Engraved brass, bouclé, leather

A family of seating built from one brass profile, engraved by hand and bent into rings, legs and rails.

The pieces share a single tube section and a single engraving pattern, so a pouf, a dining chair and an armchair read as one collection in any room. Upholstery stays pale and textured, to let the metal carry the detail.

About

Hadis Narimani is a commercial interior designer working on retail and hospitality interiors at concept stage.

Her method is technique transfer. She takes the construction logic of a garment or a craft, such as beadwork, couching or wrapping, and translates it into spatial hierarchy, material roles and atmosphere.

She trained in the UK and works internationally.
